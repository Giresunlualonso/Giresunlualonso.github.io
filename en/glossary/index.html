F1 glossary

34 common Formula 1 terms with short, clear definitions. Terms changed by the 2026 rules are marked.

107% rule
If a driver’s Q1 time exceeds 107% of the fastest time, their participation in the race is not automatically assured. The stewards may allow them to start based on the circumstances and prior performance.
Active aerodynamics
Under the 2026 rules, the front and rear wing angles can change in designated parts of the circuit. The system provides different aerodynamic configurations for corners and straights.
Blistering
A condition in which excessive heat inside the tyre creates blisters and rubber breaks on its surface. The damage can reduce grip and tyre life.
Blue flag
During a race, it tells a lapped driver to let a faster car approaching from behind pass. Repeatedly ignoring the warning can lead to a penalty.
Degradation
The loss of tyre grip and lap-time performance as a tyre is used. Temperature, track surface and driving conditions can affect the rate of degradation.
Drag Reduction System (DRS)
Under the previous rules, this system opened the rear wing in designated zones to reduce drag and aid overtaking. DRS was removed for 2026 and replaced by Straight Mode and other new modes.
Drive-through penalty
A penalty requiring a driver to enter the pit lane, pass through without stopping, and rejoin the track while respecting the pit-lane speed limit.
Energy recovery
The process of capturing energy, such as under braking, storing it electrically and later using it to produce power. This recovered energy is managed by the hybrid power unit.
Fastest lap
The shortest lap time recorded by a driver during a race. Since 2025, no additional championship point is awarded for fastest lap.
Formation lap
The preparatory lap drivers complete from their grid positions before the race start. At its end, cars line up in their starting places.
Graining
A condition in which sliding and temperature differences cause small rubber pieces to break away and stick back onto the tyre. The uneven surface can reduce grip.
Grid penalty
A penalty that moves a driver back on the starting grid. It can be imposed for driving infringements or exceeding permitted power-unit or gearbox component limits.
Ground effect
An aerodynamic effect in which airflow between the car’s floor and the track creates low pressure that pulls the car downward. It contributes to grip through corners.
Hard tyre
The most durable of the selected dry compounds and often used for longer stints. It can provide less grip than the soft compound.
Medium tyre
A tyre that balances grip and durability between the soft and hard compounds. It can suit a range of stint lengths in a race strategy.
Motor Generator Unit-Kinetic (MGU-K)
A power-unit component that converts kinetic energy into electrical energy under braking and can convert electrical energy back into mechanical power. Electrical power and energy management play a larger role in 2026.
Overcut
A strategy in which a driver stays out after a rival pits, aiming to gain time before making their own stop. It can work when the rival struggles to warm up fresh tyres.
Parc fermé
A period or area in which cars are monitored by the FIA and the work permitted on them is restricted. Unauthorised changes can result in penalties, including a pit-lane start.
Pit stop
A stop in the pit lane for a tyre change or work on the car. A fast, safe stop can save time and help a driver gain track position.
Points system
The top 10 in a Grand Prix score from 25 points down to 1; the top eight in a Sprint score from 8 down to 1. The highest season total determines the Drivers’ or Constructors’ Champion.
Porpoising
Repeated vertical bouncing caused by changes in aerodynamic load and ride height, often along a straight. It can affect driver control and car stability.
Q1
The first elimination segment of qualifying. The slowest drivers are eliminated, while the remaining drivers advance to Q2.
Q2
The second elimination segment, contested by drivers who advanced from Q1. The slowest are eliminated and the rest move on to Q3.
Q3
The final qualifying segment, which decides pole position and the front grid places. Drivers who advance from Q2 compete to set their fastest laps.
Red flag
A signal that a session or race cannot safely continue and is stopped. During a race, drivers return to the pit lane as directed.
Safety Car
A vehicle deployed to slow and bunch up the field so a race can continue safely while a hazard is addressed. Overtaking is prohibited behind it.
Soft tyre
The softest of the three dry compounds selected for a race weekend. It generally offers more grip, but its performance can fall away sooner.
Sprint
A separate, shorter race held during a Grand Prix weekend. The top eight finishers score points.
Sprint Qualifying
The qualifying session that sets the starting order for the Sprint at a Sprint weekend. Its segments are called SQ1, SQ2 and SQ3.
Stop-and-go penalty
A penalty requiring a driver to stop at their pit position for a specified period before rejoining.
Straight mode
A 2026 active-aerodynamics mode that adjusts the wings for lower drag on designated straight sections. It is not the same system as the former DRS.
Tyre compound
The rubber formulation of a tyre, which affects its balance of grip and durability. Dry compounds range from C1 to C6, with three selected as hard, medium and soft for a race weekend.
Undercut
A strategy in which a driver pits before a rival and uses fresh tyres to set faster laps, aiming to be ahead when the rival makes their stop.
Virtual Safety Car (VSC)
A procedure that neutralises the race without deploying a physical Safety Car. Drivers must respect prescribed minimum times and may not overtake.
